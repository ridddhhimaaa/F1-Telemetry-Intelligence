2 Distance Correction & Monotonicity Audit

An audit of src/preprocessing/clean_telemetry.py was conducted to establish whether Distance_Corrected is strictly increasing (Δd > 0) or monotonically non-decreasing (Δd ≥ 0), and whether repeated distance coordinates corrupt or reflect legitimate vehicle behaviour.

Mathematical & Empirical Findings: Monotonically Non-Decreasing (Δd ≥ 0)

The audit proves conclusively that Distance_Corrected is monotonically non-decreasing across all 96 driver-lap sequences. Exactly 152 telemetry samples exhibit consecutive differences where Δd = 0.0m:

  • 91 samples across Lap 1 (all 20 drivers): Cars parked stationary on their respective starting grid slots awaiting the start lights (Speed = 0.0 km/h, Throttle = 0%). In addition, negative grid coordinates clamped to 0.0m create consecutive 0.0m plateau points. This represents legitimate physical vehicle state.
  • 27 samples on Franco Colapinto (COL) Lap 4: Sub-millimetre GPS timing loop jitter where raw distance momentarily dropped by -0.0001m. Clamping to running maximum held the distance coordinate constant (Δd = 0.0m).
  • 34 samples on Oscar Piastri (PIA) Lap 5: Floating point precision jitter where raw distance dropped by -0.003m, held constant by the monotonic running maximum filter.

3 Spatial Resampling Audit (495,849 vs 501,695 Rows)

An in-depth investigation was executed to resolve why resampled_telemetry.parquet contains 495,849 rows across 95 driver-laps rather than the theoretical maximum of 501,695 rows (95 × 5,281 positions).

The Engineering Explanation for the 5,846 Row Difference

The theoretical calculation of 501,695 rows assumes that every driver covers exactly 5,281.0 metres (from 0.0m to 5,280.0m inclusive) on every single lap. In actual Formula 1 telemetry:

  1. Racing Line Geometry: The official circuit length (5,281.0m) is measured along the geometric centerline of the track. Professional drivers do not drive the centerline; they take the racing line, cutting apexes and minimizing trajectory radius. Consequently, a driver's integrated wheel and GPS distance at the start-finish line typically ranges between 5,215m and 5,260m (mean: 5,219.46m).
  2. Strict Anti-Extrapolation Policy: resample_telemetry.py strictly bounds the uniform grid within observed telemetry:
    grid_start = np.ceil(max(0.0, d_min))
    grid_end = np.floor(min(5281.0, d_max))
    If the grid were arbitrarily forced to extend to 5,280.0m, the interpolation algorithm would be forced to extrapolate unobserved data for an average of 61.5 metres per lap past the finish line, fabricating non-existent telemetry.
  3. Exclusion of Perez Lap 1: Sergio Perez completed only 3,932.8m before his collision retirement (< 4,800.0m threshold). He is correctly excluded (96 - 1 = 95 laps).

Conclusion: The 495,849 row count is physically and mathematically correct. Forcing extra records into the dataset would violate data integrity.
